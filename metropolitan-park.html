The ground floor was the negotiated part

01

Central Green, two dog runs, a Children's Garden

[2.5 ACRES]

James Corner Field Operations — the practice behind New York's High Line — laid out the landscape. The lawn takes markets, film nights and county programming; the edges hold more than 50,000 plants and over 175 trees, most of them native. Everything sits at grade, with no step between the sidewalk and the grass.

02

Queen City, 2022 — Nekisha Durrett

[903 VESSELS]

In 1941 the federal government seized Black-owned land in Arlington to build the Pentagon. Queen City went with it — homes, churches, businesses gone within the year — and for eight decades nothing on the ground said it had been there.

Durrett's answer is a brick tower in the corner of the park. Brick, concrete, ceramic pottery and steel; the plaque calls it standing tall and vigilant. Held within it are 903 handmade ceramic vessels, made in the shape and colour of a drop of water, one for each displaced resident. She commissioned Black ceramicists from the local area and beyond, and worked with historians, living descendants of Queen City residents, and local art spaces.

It stands in a park paid for by the largest corporate land deal in the county's history, a mile from the building that erased what it commemorates. That is the argument, not an accident of siting.

03

Merlin & Jasper — HQ2, phase one

[2.1M SQ FT]

Two 22-storey towers, 327 feet, delivered in June 2023 and certified LEED v4 Platinum — ZGF, the architect, calls it the largest LEED v4 Platinum project in the world. Fifty thousand square feet of the ground floor is retail rather than lobby, and the buildings meet the park at grade through garage-style doors that open onto the lawn in good weather. About 8,500 people work at HQ2 as of 2026, against a commitment of at least 25,000 jobs at a minimum average wage of $150,000.

04

Independent stores

[NOT A FOOD COURT]

Amazon reserved the retail for small operators — fourteen of the businesses in phase one are minority-owned or women-owned — and that decision is the difference between a plaza and a street. Good Company Doughnuts, Maman, Makers Union, Taqueria Xochi, Peruvian Brothers, South Block, Toby's, MoCA Arlington's studio and Conte's Bike Shop all face the lawn. But the ground floor of a headquarters is still the ground floor of a headquarters. The one worth a detour is a block west.

Gwenie's Pastries

[1417 S. FERN ST]

Gwendolyn Fernandez started Gwenie's in Rockville about twenty years ago. Her children, Stella and Javier, opened this one in September 2025 — the family's first Virginia storefront, and a two-business room: Gwenie's on the pastry side, Lapu Lapu on the savoury side, one counter each.

Gwenie's is ube cheesecake, cakes and pies, and filled donuts — ube, mango, pandan, Nutella. Lapu Lapu is Filipino breakfast sandwiches on pandesal made in-house, ube soft serve and specialty lattes. Breakfast runs all day, which is the correct policy.

05

PenPlace, and the Helix

[ON HOLD]

The County Board approved phase two in April 2022: three more office buildings on the parcel north of the park, plus the Helix, a spiralling tower with a planted exterior walkway meant to be the campus's landmark. Amazon paused construction in 2023 and has not restarted. In June 2025 the County extended the site plan, pushing the deadline to begin construction to June 30, 2028.

You can see the site from the north edge of the park. It is worth looking at, because the park you are standing in is what the finished version is supposed to feel like.
