Lost Dog Cafe

The restaurant funded the shelter, not the other way round.

Lost Dog Cafe opened on Washington Boulevard in Westover in 1985. The name is not decoration. Pam McAlwee ran dog adoption listings using the restaurant's own telephone number, and in 2001 the owners bought sixty-three acres in Fauquier County for the rescue the business had grown into. In 2026 that rescue marked twenty-five years.

Gourmet Pizza Deli

[ 01 ]

It opened in 1985 as a small deli selling pizza, beer and wine.

Ross Underwood and Pam McAlwee established Lost Dog Cafe in the Westover shopping village in 1985. The cafe's own account describes the starting point as a small, unassuming wine, beer and cheese shop; the Washington Post and the rescue foundation describe it opening as the Gourmet Pizza Deli. These are the same business rather than competing histories — the Westover restaurant still trades as Lost Dog Café & Gourmet Pizza Deli, and the foundation's own page splits the difference, calling it a cozy deli with pizza, beer and wine.

What is not in dispute is the year, the founders, and the location: a modest storefront on Washington Boulevard in a pre-war commercial village, doing counter service.

The phone number

[ 02 ]

Adoption ads ran on the restaurant's own line.

The detail that explains everything downstream is this: McAlwee listed dogs for adoption in the classifieds and printed the cafe's telephone number as the contact. Someone calling about a dog reached the restaurant. Someone calling about a pizza reached the same phone.

Her reasoning was operational rather than sentimental. She was working Friday, Saturday and Sunday — the days families go looking for a dog — so the restaurant's line was the number where she could actually be reached.

That is what a rescue looks like before it has an organization around it: a person doing the work with whatever infrastructure they already have, which in this case was a sandwich shop's landline. The Lost Dog and Cat Rescue Foundation grew out of it, and in 2001 the owners bought sixty-three acres at Sumerduck, in Fauquier County, to house the operation.

The food

[ 03 ]

Pizza, sandwiches, and a beer list that outgrew the room.

The menu is long — pizzas, a large sandwich list, salads — and the beer selection is treated with more seriousness than a neighborhood pizza place strictly requires. The Westover room is casual to the point of plainness, with the patio doing most of the work in warm months.

It is a family restaurant in the operational sense: children are unremarkable there, the noise floor is high enough that nobody notices, and the food arrives fast. For a household with young children in north Arlington, that combination is worth more than a tasting menu.

Four rooms

[ 04 ]

The Columbia Pike location closed in October 2025 after sixteen years.

Lost Dog expanded well beyond Westover over the years. In October 2025 the Columbia Pike location at 2920 Columbia Pike closed after sixteen years, following months of declining sales during construction on the corridor. Four locations remain: Westover, McLean, Dunn Loring and Alexandria.

This page notes the closure because a guide that only records openings is not a guide, it is an advertisement. The Westover room is the one that has been there since 1985, and it is the one this entry is about.

Where

5876 Washington Boulevard, Arlington, VA 22205 — Westover.

In the Westover shopping village, across from Westover Market. Street parking on the surrounding blocks; the village lot fills on weekend evenings.

Hours

Around 11 a.m. to 9 p.m. Sunday through Thursday and to 9:30 p.m. Friday and Saturday. The patio is the seat worth having between April and October.
